## Hazardous Weather Conditions

Maintain visual contact with RPA and communicate the situation to the Flight Crew

1. Verify:
   - Check state of RPA (Status/Flight Mode)
   - Check RPA location/altitude
2. Take Actions:
   - Ground Crew: Review weather forecast
   - Visual Observer: Monitor changing weather conditions
   - PIC: Evaluate if conditions exceed safe operating requirements
3. Issues:
   - If weather conditions are changing rapidly: Maneuver the RPA to a safe divert location, land immediately
   - Err on the side of caution: Maneuver the RPA closer to PIC to evaluate conditions
4. Post-Incident:
   - Document incident

## Low Battery or Status Error

Maintain visual contact with RPA and communicate the situation to the Flight Crew

1. Verify:
  - Check state of RPA (Status/Flight Mode)
  - Check location of RPA in flight area
2. Take Actions:
  - Ground Crew: Alert flight area
  - Visual Observer: Monitor air traffic and contact ATC if necessary
  - PIC: Determine safest landing location. Land as soon as possible.
3. Issues:
  - If disoriented: Use 'Return to Home' to bring RPA back for landing, regain visual contact as soon as possible
  - If strong winds prevent return flight: Use camera to locate suitable divert location
4. Post-Incident:
  - Document incident
  - Recovery telemetry logs
  - Inspect RPA and battery for physical signs of damage

## Collision with Hazard

Maintain visual contact with RPA and communicate the situation to the Flight Crew

1. Verify:
  - Check state of RPA (Status/Flight Mode)
  - Check RPA location/altitude
2. Take Actions:
  - Ground Crew: Alert and clear flight area, prepare safety equipment
  - Visual Observer: Guide PIC around hazard
  - PIC: Establish positive control and land immediately
3. Issues:
  - If the RPA regains stability and control: Maneuver to a safe divert location, land immediately
  - If the RPA does not regain stability: Initiate manual motor shutdown
4. Post-Incident:
  - Document incident
  - Inspect RPA and battery for physical signs of damage

## Fly Away/Loss of GPS

Maintain visual contact with RPA and communicate the situation to the Flight Crew

1. Verify:
  - Check state of RPA (Status/Flight Mode)
  - Check RPA location/altitude
  - Check transmitter/tablet status and control links
2. Take Actions:
  - Ground Crew: Alert and clear flight area, prepare safety equipment
  - Visual Observer: Monitor air traffic and contact ATC if necessary
  - PIC: Follow RPA and attempt to re-establish connection/positive control
3. Issues:
  - If control is active, and RPA is drifting or moving incorrectly: Lower altitude and attempt to keep RPA stationary, try 'Return to Home' to bring RPA back for landing, try switching control to 'manual' or attitude control
  - If control is active but RPA is not responding: Try switching control to 'manual' or attitude control
  - If control is not active: Assist Ground Crew in alerting and clearing flight area
4. Post-Incident:
  - Document incident
  - Recovery telemetry logs
  - Inspect RPA and battery for physical signs of damage

  - Check transmitter/tablet status and control links

4. Post-Incident:
  - Document incident
  - Recovery telemetry logs
  - Inspect RPA and battery for physical signs of damage



## In-Flight or Post-Flight Fire

Maintain visual contact with RPA and communicate the situation to the Flight Crew

1. Verify:
  - Check state of RPA (Status/Flight Mode)
  - Check RPA location/altitude
  - Check transmitter/tablet status and control links
2. Take Actions:
  - Ground Crew: Alert and clear flight area, prepare safety equipment
  - Visual Observer: Be prepared to call emergency services
  - PIC: Immediately terminate flight
3. Issues:
  - If RPA sparks a ground fire: Secure site and attempt to extinguish the fire with Ground Crew
  - If unable to extinguish fire: Call emergency services, stop all fire suppression efforts and begin minimizing potential damage by clearing the area and removing other potential fire hazards
4. Post-Incident:
  - Document incident
  - Contact PI and Designated Local Authority

## Pilot Incapacitation

Ensure the PIC is IMMEDIATELY attended to. 

1. Typically, the designated VO will be the closest person to the PIC
2. Communicate the situation to the Flight Crew
3. Take Actions:
  - VO to pause or stop flight operation, then resume attending to PIC
  - Ground Crew must be prepared to call emergency services
  - Check transmitter/tablet status and control links
4. Issues:
  - Call emergency services (911) if there is a possibility that they may be needed for the situation
  - If flight operation cannot be paused or stopped, initiate a manual termination, unless an alternative operator is available.
5. RESPONSE PRIORITIES:
  - Avoid delaying emergency services to PIC
  - Ensure RPA will not place additional persons at risk
  - Prevent the RPA from causing property damage
  - If able, land the RPA safely
6. Post-Incident:
  - Document incident
  - Contact any Designated Local Authority as required

## Airspace Encroachment

Maintain visual contact with RPA and communicate to the Flight Crew if applicable.

1. Verify:
  - Check state of RPA (Status/Flight Mode)
  - Check location of RPA in flight area
2. Take Actions:
  - Ground Crew: Alert flight area, prepare for landing
  - Visual Observer: Monitor air traffic and provide
  - PIC: Evaluate best course of action
3. Issues:
  - If a collision is imminent: PIC must take immediate evasive action
  - If a collision is a strong possibility: PIC must land or terminate the flight at the nearest divert or safe location
  - If the encroachment is unsafe but not likely to result in a collision: PIC must pause flight, reduce flight altitude and monitor the situation. PIC may only resume flight operation when the hazard has passed
  - If the encroachment is noticeable, but not likely to cause a safety issue: PIC may continue flight operations
4. Post-Incident:
  - Document incident
  - Review See & Avoid procedures
